02 · My Role
Product Lead, Not Just Designer

Leading the Team

I served as the Product Lead for a 5-person team (Frontend, Backend, QA, and UI). I managed stakeholder requirements from HR, HRBPs, and leadership, prioritized core features, and defined the product roadmap. We operated using a Scrum framework with short, iterative sprints.

The Core Contribution

My core contribution was breaking down repetitive manual tasks and delegating them to the system, while carefully identifying which stages required human judgment. We built our automation layer directly onto Lark's (Feishu) Calendar and Messaging APIs, integrating with tools they already used ensured high adoption without a platform switch.

03 · The Problem
The Bottleneck Was Coordination, Not Interviews

Every step of the hiring process relied on HR pushing it forward. With 100+ open roles, tasks frequently slipped through the cracks. The bottleneck wasn't the interviews themselves, it was the coordination surrounding them.

Manual Coordination at Every Stage

HR manually triggered every pipeline step: moving candidates, sending notifications, chasing feedback. With 100+ open roles at once, things fell through the cracks. Hiring timelines stretched not because the process was slow, but because someone had to remember to advance it.

Panel Scheduling Was a Black Hole

Coordinating a multi-panel interview involved manually checking 3–5 people's calendars, endless back-and-forth in group chats, and chasing feedback post-interview. This coordination overhead consumed most of HR's working day.

Delayed Feedback Lost Top Candidates

Hiring managers were deep in projects. Without automatic reminders, feedback sat incomplete for days. By the time HR followed up, strong candidates had already accepted other offers. Crucial information was scattered across group chats, making it nearly impossible to track.

Core Strategy · Solution 1 of 3

Panel Interview Automation

Eliminating manual coordination for panel interviews, the biggest time drain

Smart Interview Scheduling

  • A three-person panel interview used to drain HR's time: checking calendars, negotiating slots, and following up on feedback. I automated this entirely.
  • The system reads interviewers' Lark calendars to recommend available slots. Once confirmed, it sends automated invites, with all necessary information, to the candidate, every interviewer, and HR.
  • By integrating with tools they already used, we ensured high adoption rates. HR and hiring managers didn't have to switch to a new platform to get things done.

Role-Based Interview Templates

  • I implemented role-based interview templates, portfolio reviews for designers, technical screening for engineers, moving away from ad-hoc planning for every candidate.
  • HR configures the full interview sequence per role type upfront. Different roles need very different interview structures, and the template system captures this without repeated setup.

Standardized Evaluation & Same-Day Decisions

  • Immediately after the interview, the system sends each interviewer a direct message with a link to the feedback form. I unified the scoring metrics across each role, replacing freeform notes that were impossible to compare across candidates.
  • Upon the final interviewer's submission, HR receives a consolidated view of all evaluations, enabling same-day decisions.
Supporting Pillar 1 · Solution 2 of 3

Automated Hiring Pipelines

HR sets the rules once, the system drives the rest

Automated Recruitment Flows

  • HR sets the rules once: if a candidate passes, the system advances them, notifies stakeholders, schedules the next action, or sends a rejection letter automatically.
  • Pass moves forward. Fail sends a rejection. Nobody has to chase anyone. This transforms reactive, manual work into a system-driven process.

Unified Sourcing Inbox

  • We consolidated sourcing, LinkedIn, referrals, and campus hiring, into a unified inbox with smart labeling to filter out unqualified candidates and de-duplication to prevent contacting the same person twice.
  • Previously, HR logged into each platform separately to refresh listings and download resumes. Now it's one action: publish everywhere, receive everything in one place.

Smart Screening & Deduplication

  • With 100+ resumes arriving per role per day, the first problem is volume. Smart tagging flags candidates who don't meet hard requirements so HR can focus on qualified ones without reading everything.
  • Cross-channel deduplication catches when the same person applied through multiple platforms, preventing duplicate outreach and candidate confusion.
Supporting Pillar 2 · Solution 3 of 3

Talent Pool & Data Analytics

Turn short-term hiring into long-term talent assets

Talent Pool Management

  • Qualified but unselected candidates are now automatically stored in a searchable database, retaining their past evaluations for future use.
  • When a similar position opens later, HR can surface and re-engage them in one click, eliminating the need to start sourcing from scratch every time.

Automated Analytics Dashboard

  • Our automated dashboard tracks key KPIs, dropout rates per stage, slow-feedback departments, and offer acceptance rates, eliminating the need for manual reporting.
  • The dashboard answers the questions HR actually asks: where are candidates dropping off? Which departments have the slowest feedback turnaround? Which channels produce the best offer acceptance rates?
07 · Key Iterations
What Changed Along the Way
01

From "Full Customization" to "Template + Custom"

My initial "blank canvas" flow editor overwhelmed users during testing. The empty canvas felt more like a burden than empowerment, users stalled. I pivoted to a template-first model: start from a preset template covering the most common hiring flows, then customize only what was necessary. Most users defaulted to templates and only adjusted the parts that differed. Configuration time dropped from 15 minutes to under 3 minutes.

02

Boosting Feedback Rates with Direct Links

Low submission rates were due to friction, not negligence. Interviewers weren't skipping on purpose, they were simply forgetting, or couldn't find the form when they did remember. By adding an automated "nudge" with a direct link to the feedback form via Lark, submission rates climbed by 60%. No searching, no extra steps, just tap and submit.

08 · Reflections
What I Learned

Not Everything Should Be Automated

HR needs a sense of control. I learned to keep the human in the loop for critical decision-making while delegating the "mechanical" coordination to the system. Good automation should make people feel lighter, not redundant.

"What They Ask For" vs. "What They Need"

Stakeholders often suggest specific features rather than expressing their actual pain points. I learned to dig deeper into the "why" before designing, ensuring we solve the root problem, not just the symptom.

Leveraging AI for Product Management

If I were to rebuild this today, I would use AI to structure interview transcripts into functional logic, generate prototype flows, and map API field requirements, accelerating the tedious parts of the development lifecycle to allow more time for high-level design decisions.
