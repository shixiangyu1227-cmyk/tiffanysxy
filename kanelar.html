Kanelär — App Walkthrough
Background

Why I made this

When I was taking SFI (the Swedish language course for immigrants) in Stockholm, I picked up new words every day on the street, on the subway, in cafés.

I tried the mainstream language learning apps. I opened Duolingo, and first an owl was nagging me to do my daily tasks, then came the streak flames, the ads, the leaderboards.

But what I actually wanted to do in that moment was very simple: save the word I just heard.

I realized that for someone already living inside the language, what they need isn't a course that starts from zero. They need a vocabulary notebook that can keep up with the pace of daily life.

That's how Kanelär came about.

Use case

Tuesday morning, on the subway

Two Swedes next to me are chatting, and one word keeps coming up: "fika."

Open Kanelär, search fika, see the definition "coffee break" with the example sentence "Vi tar en fika klockan tre." (We're having a fika at three.)
Add it to my word list.
On the way home from work, tap "Start learning," flip through the words I added today, mark the ones I know as "mastered" and the ones I didn't as "practice again."
The number on the home screen goes +1.

That's it.

Design

A few design decisions

Four numbers only
The home screen shows only four numbers: total words, practicing, mastered, added today.
Big and quiet
The numbers are big and quiet, so you can see your progress at a glance, without notifications or streaks demanding your attention.
Capture-first flow
The whole app is designed around a single action: saving the word you just heard, as fast as possible, before the moment passes.
The name

Why a cinnamon roll

I asked myself: what's something Swedes touch every day, that newcomers run into right away, and that has a bit of warmth to it?

A cinnamon roll. Sweden even has a Cinnamon Roll Day (October 4th). It's the most everyday piece of bread in this country, and one of the first Swedish nouns everyone learns.

That's where the name came from: Kanel (cinnamon) + lär (learn) = Kanelär.

Made in Stockholm, with cinnamon. 🥐
